# Customer discovery

## Research conducted

- 30 pet-owner survey responses
- Four follow-up interviews with pet owners
- One interview with veterinary staff

## Survey findings

| Question area | Result | Share of relevant respondents |
|---|---:|---:|
| Experienced difficulty affording veterinary care | 19 of 30 | 63% |
| Looked for financial assistance | 16 of 30 | 53% |
| Had difficulty determining eligibility after searching | 11 of 16 | 69% |
| Abandoned at least one application after searching | 7 of 16 | 44% |

The last two rows refer specifically to the 16 people who had searched for assistance. Percentages are rounded to whole numbers. These were discovery interviews and surveys, not a representative national study.

## What changed

The initial concept focused on predicting veterinary costs. Customer discovery highlighted a different question: once an owner receives a bill, **which funding options are relevant and how does the owner act on them?**

That led to a more concrete product direction:

- Start from a treatment estimate and the amount the owner can pay.
- Check assistance and financing conditions before presenting next steps.
- Explain when a program is excluded, possible, or requires more information.
- Help prepare the information needed to apply or speak with the clinic.

## What to test next

The survey establishes a reason to investigate eligibility confusion and application abandonment. It does not identify one universal reason why every application was abandoned. Follow-up work will examine specific failure points, including program rules, documents, response times, credit preferences, and uncertainty about qualification.

The next evaluation will compare whether owners can identify a suitable financial route more accurately and efficiently with a structured case-screening approach than with existing resources.

*Only aggregate customer-discovery findings are published here. Participant-level records and identifying details are kept private.*
